AI-generated Journey Campaigns

Every brand gets the same eleven starter journey emails. An AI drafts them from this page, in the brand's voice, and Marketing reviews and refines every draft in the campaign builder before anything goes live.

Direction: Kaylinn Brophy (Marketing), 2026-11-07 · Vocabulary and the day-365 state settled 2026-11-08 · Step names from her brief 2026-11-09 · This page is the requirements record the drafts are built to; the evidence brief is the case for the intervals. Where this page departs from her brief, the departure is in red.

What the drafts are, and how they get made

Eleven drafts per brand

One draft automation per step, on that step's own list (never the newsletter list), tagged journey:<series>:<day>. The Preview button on Brands > Usage (F9) renders all of them in the journey guide's layout, so the whole journey can be read as one piece before any step is switched on. Until Marketing starts a step's automation and switches its list's entry trigger on, the Usage pane shows the step grey: a draft whose list has no trigger yet, so nobody is joined.

Three No-purchase steps, four Post-purchase, four Re-engagement. Thirsty Girl's set was the first (2026-11-02) and was rebuilt to this direction on 2026-11-10 as nine drafts.

Note to KaylinnThirsty Girl's rebuild has nine drafts, not eleven: day 0 of both series is already your own campaign (the running Welcome and the running first-order Thank-you), so no AI draft was made for either, and the earlier day-0 drafts were deleted. That is a judgment call; say if you want AI drafts of those two as well.

What the AI may use

Only what can be substantiated: the brand's own sent campaigns, its website, approved claims and product information, and offers that already exist. Anything it cannot verify is left as a bracketed fact for Marketing to fill, such as [shipping window], never invented.

Most brands are new. Where there are no reviews or social proof, the draft leans on product discovery, education and existing offers instead of forcing a concept.

Marketing owns the final word

Drafts are starting points. Marketing edits them in the builder, and nothing sends until Marketing starts the step's automation. Once a review has begun, the drafts are never regenerated over the top of the edits.

Each draft carries a recommended subject line, preview text, headline, body copy, one CTA and a suggested visual direction.

Rules for every draft

  • One objective, at most one primary CTA per email (the Thank-you may have none). Each step has a distinct purpose that supports a first or a repeat purchase, or retention.
  • Never repeat the previous step's message. A journey read end to end should feel like a sequence, not the same email three times.
  • Follow the brand's own voice and positioning. Concise, engaging, easy to skim on a phone.
  • Only approved claims, product information, testimonials and existing offers. No invented urgency, reviews, resources, promotions or website functionality.
  • Evergreen: the copy must still be true in six months and must stay separate from the month's broadcasts (the evidence brief's item 4).
  • Do not request a review anywhere in the journey. The sites have no review functionality.
  • Do not duplicate the order confirmation. Komigo already sends it. Note to KaylinnYour brief says AfterShip handles the order confirmation. It does not: AfterShip sends only the shipping-status emails (shipped, out for delivery, delivered). The order confirmation is Komigo's.
  • Every link is a tracked, personalised link through the attribute picker or Personalize product link, never a hand-typed ?ref= (the shop link standard).

The series names are the canonical ones (Kaylinn's brief uses the same three in its header strip). No-purchase series (Welcome series)

newsletter join → any paid order of the brand, before or after joining, exits the steps

Days 0, 2 and 5. The goal of the whole series is a first purchase, without artificial urgency and without a dedicated welcome offer unless the brand already has one. Someone who has ever bought from the brand is not a non-buyer: a prior customer who joins gets the Day 0 welcome and none of the steps.

Post-purchase series (New Customer series)

first paid order ever with the brand, subscribed at the time

Day 0, then delivery-based steps. The clock is the AfterShip delivery confirmation: "Getting Started" the day the package is reported delivered, the next two steps 9 and 25 days after it. A tracked package still in transit waits up to 10 days after the order; after that, or with no delivery confirmation, the steps fall back to the order date plus 5, 14 and 30 days, which is where the ~5d, ~14d and ~30d labels come from.

Re-engagement series (Sunset flow)

no click, Gmail-confirmed open or purchase for 180 days, after at least 6 broadcasts since sign-up or the last return from a hold

Regular sends stop the day someone is held (day 180), and the first ask goes out that same day. The second and third follow at +90 and +180 days from the hold, then the confirmation at +185 (days 270, 360 and 365 at the default window). Each is an automation Marketing builds per brand, every brand onboarded with all four; the Usage pane shows each step's automations by state (green running, amber paused, grey draft or scheduled, a red 0 for none), and the date schedule runs regardless.

Qualifying engagement, and where Manage Preferences fits

Kaylinn's rule for the day-360 email is that its CTA must connect to an action the system recognises as qualifying engagement. This is what the system recognises.

Changed 2026-11-09Unsubscribe now means the brand, not the list. One click in any of a brand's emails — a welcome, a step, a Thank-you, a re-engagement ask, a broadcast — unsubscribes the person from every marketing list of that brand: the newsletter, the journey step lists, the first-order and SKU trigger lists, the re-engagement queues and any imported segment. Other brands are untouched, and so is anything flagged transactional (except the list that email itself was sent from). Unticking a brand on the Manage Preferences page does exactly the same. Two consequences worth knowing: the ask opt-out above is recorded on the brand even when the person has already moved past the queue that ask came from; and "never asked again" means on that consent — if they genuinely sign up again, a further year of silence starts the clock afresh.

So for the day-360 email: the primary button is a tracked link to the brand site (one click, done). Manage Preferences is a legitimate second route, two steps long: the page shows the brand unticked from day 180 on, and ticking it resumes sends. After day 365 the same tick is the way back.

What the page shows (since 2026-11-08): under a held brand's unticked box, a Paused note (regular sends stopped; tick the box to resume) or, after day 365, a Stopped note (a year without activity; tick the box to resume), so nobody reads a hold as an unsubscribe they never made. The tick is the resume; there is no separate button.

Suppression, not unsubscribe: the day-365 state

Kaylinn's reject note on policy item 22 (2026-11-05) made two asks. Both are adopted; this is how.

1 · Record it as an inactivity suppression

Accepted in full. The day-365 state is called inactivity suppression everywhere: policy item 22, the policy table, the journey guide's day-365 step, and the confirmation email, which says the brand has stopped sending marketing emails rather than "you've been unsubscribed".

The industry draws the same line. An unsubscribe is consent the person revoked and only they can restore; a suppression is a sender-applied stop the sender can lift. Klaviyo, Braze and HubSpot all run their sunset flows as suppressions, never as unsubscribes. Google and Yahoo ask senders to consider stopping mail to the unengaged; neither says how to record it.

The data already kept the two apart: a held or suppressed row carries a hold record with its reason and date; a real opt-out is a separate release to "unsubscribed". Only the words were wrong.

2 · Don't suppress before the re-engagement emails are activated

Met as a commitment, not a mechanism. Keying the suppression on the asks was the earlier design, retired because it left a brand with no automations holding people forever and let any hand broadcast reset the clock. The date rule stays.

What the ask protects is the person's chance to answer, and that is met in practice: the earliest day-360 cohort is March 2027 (the September 2026 holds plus 180 days); the Usage pane counts each step's automations by state (green running, amber paused, grey draft or scheduled, and a red 0 where a brand has none); and a Gate Watch human gate holds Marketing to all four automations running on every brand before the first cohort reaches day 360 (every brand is onboarded with sunset on; the Usage pane's Sunset column and Re-engagement circles show where that did not happen). Note to KaylinnJourney drafts exist for Thirsty Girl only until the per-brand generator is built; every other brand's re-engagement automations are Marketing's to write until then.

By day 365 a person has had no regular sends for 185 days, only the three asks, so the suppression changes no deliverability outcome. It is bookkeeping plus the door back.
