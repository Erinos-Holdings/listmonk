One Journey, Every Brand

The adopted standard: one engagement journey on every brand, 11 steps in three series, approved by Marketing item by item (Kaylinn Brophy, from 2026-11-05). This page is the evidence behind each interval and the record of where each proposed change ended up.

Related: Brands › Usage (the journey guide and the Lifecycle series decisions, the approved text) · AI-generated Journey Campaigns (the copy direction the step drafts are built to) · Shop Link Attribution (the standard every link in a step follows)

The standard

One journey skeleton for every brand; brands vary only the copy and the offers. The intervals start on data-proven, industry-backed anchors, and an interval changes only when the numbers prove it should, for every brand at once. The rest of this page is the case for that, including the defensible alternatives and where each landed.

9 of 13rules sit on a Klaviyo default or published recommendation; the other 4 are deliberate, stated extensions

Why one standard rather than a path per brand

No single brand can test its own intervals

To tell a 2.0% conversion step from a 2.5% one (a 25% lift) at normal confidence takes about 14,000 people per arm. Our brands sign up far fewer than that in a month, so a per-brand interval test would run for years before it said anything. Pooled across brands, the same test finishes in months.

The industry already standardizes

Klaviyo ships one pre-built Welcome, Post-purchase and Sunset flow to every account, and its "three emails over a week" welcome guidance comes from analysis across thousands of its ecommerce customers, not per-store tuning. Brands differentiate on copy, product and offer. The skeleton is shared because the behaviour it responds to (a signup cooling off, a parcel arriving, a reader going quiet) is the same for a pouch brand and a skincare brand.

Comparability is the whole point of Brand Usage

The Usage pane scores every brand on the same leaves and the Matrix compares them side by side. That only means something if Day 5 is Day 5 everywhere. With a standard, a result on one brand is a lesson for all of them; with per-brand paths, every brand is a sample of one and nothing transfers.

It is also the only way the copy stays maintainable: today's standard is already 11 steps × up to 5 languages per brand.

Changed 2026-11-09An unsubscribe now means the brand, not the list. When someone unsubscribes from any of a brand's emails — or unticks the brand on the Manage Preferences page — they come off every marketing list of that brand: the newsletter, the journey step lists, the first-order and SKU trigger lists, the re-engagement queues and any imported segment. Other brands are untouched, and lists flagged transactional are not touched at all. This changes nothing about the suppression-versus-unsubscribe line above: a hold is still ours to lift and an unsubscribe is still the person's own revoked consent — it is now revoked for the whole brand, which is what the word means to the person reading the email. The same rule was applied backwards to the brands' history.

Re-engagement: why 180 days, not 90

Ninety days is the common starting point and we are not disputing it. Klaviyo's default engaged segments are 30, 60 and 90 days, and its staff advise 90 for brands that send weekly or monthly. We hold at 180 for four reasons, and we keep the ability to move back to 90 per brand without a deploy.

1 · Our engagement test is stricter than the one 90 days assumes

Most 90-day segments count any open. Apple Mail Privacy Protection fires opens automatically, inflating open rates by a reported 20–40%; Klaviyo's own advice is to exclude those opens. We go further: only a click, a paid order or a Gmail-confirmed open counts. A subscriber judged on that bar deserves more calendar time, and Klaviyo's never-engaged sunset segment itself uses 180 days.

2 · Purging fast loses real readers

Return Path's study of 33 retailer win-back programmes (300M emails) found 45% of win-back recipients went on to read later messages, on average 57 days after the win-back, some up to 300 days later. Only 12% read the win-back itself. A 90-day cut plus a short sunset would have removed many of them.

3 · The Younique win-back is our own data point

46,272sent to buyers lapsed >90 days
30.6%viewed (14,162 people)
2.8%clicked (1,307 people)

A lapsed audience still contained a reading third. It also showed the limit: once the day-4 FBL complaint rate on a tranche passed 0.1%, sending was paused the same day and the campaign cancelled, with 26,759 unreached people archived rather than mailed. Both halves of that argue for 180 watched closely rather than 90 left alone.

4 · We see trouble days before Google reports it

Postmaster Tools lags two to three days. We do not wait for it: FBL complaints and hard, soft and block-class bounces per campaign the same day; per-client view rates on every send, so a Gmail drop against a steady Yahoo is visible before Postmaster moves; hourly Brand Health per brand (Postmaster compliance and spam rate, SES account reputation, blocklists), trust-tiered open counts, and a sunset run that skips a brand whose tracking looks broken. The 180-day window is a per-brand registry value: shortening one brand to 90 is a one-line change, not a release, and the whole re-engagement schedule follows the hold, keeping its +90, +180 and +185-day spacing.

Our commitment (item 17): if complaint rates rise, we'll be obligated to shorten the window out of necessity. We watch each brand's trend, not one-off breaches (campaigns cross 0.1% now and then and recover): a brand developing a sustained rise that we confirm comes from its >90-day cohort gets a shorter window. The window is liberal because the exits are fast.

Defensible alternatives

These are the positions a well-read challenger would take. Each is reasonable; here is where we stand on each.

Three welcome emails, not four. Adopted 2026-11-06.

Three over about a week (Day 0 · 3 · 7) is Klaviyo's default and its best-supported recommendation. Our Day 10 nudge added a conversion attempt the evidence did not demand; this page called it the interval we would concede first, and Marketing conceded it: the No-purchase series is Day 0 · 2 · 5 on every brand.

Review ask timed by product category. Moot since 2026-11-06.

Several practitioners argue consumables want a 7-day ask and durable goods 14–21. The journey sends no review request (the sites have no review functionality, item 9). The same argument applies to the Day 14 educational email, and there it is a copy decision inside a standard step rather than a reason for per-brand intervals; the delivery-based clock (modification 4) removes most of the timing error.

A 90-day hold for high-frequency brands.

Klaviyo advises 30 days for daily senders and 90 for weekly. If a brand moves to several broadcasts a week, 180 is too generous for it and the registry window should come down, with the whole re-engagement schedule moving with it. Nothing in the standard prevents that.

Smart Sending that also suppresses journey steps.

Klaviyo's pre-built flows have Smart Sending on by default, so a flow email can be skipped after a campaign. We exempt journeys because each step sends once, ever, and a skipped step is a lost step. If Marketing prefers Klaviyo's behaviour, it is the same rule with journeys included.

Fewer, earlier re-engagement asks.

Some teams compress the sunset to three asks within 30 days and suppress. That is cheaper to run. We prefer spreading the asks across the back half of the year because of the Return Path finding that re-reads arrive months later, and because the 360/365 lists are locked to automations so nobody past day 360 can be promoted to by mistake.

Revising the standard

We would revise an interval, not just a copy line, on any of these: a per-brand A/B with at least 14,000 subscribers per arm showing a different step wins; a step's unsubscribe share consistently above the share of the step before it; the welcome series' Day 2 or Day 5 clicks falling below the broadcast average for a quarter; or a held-at-180 cohort whose re-engagement asks draw complaints above 0.1%. Bring any of those and the standard moves for every brand at once, which is the point of having one.
